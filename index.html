A fictional composite journey through the Kokoda campaign of 1942. Carry a message across the mountains and remember that survival was never only about fighting. Dedicated to the Australian militia remembered as the “Chocolate Soldiers”, to Ted Sylvester Daly, and to the Papuan people whose knowledge and care were essential.

Inspired by the Australian and Papuan stories of Kokoda

“Chocolate Soldiers” was a nickname for Australian militia; it began as a slight, though some later wore it with pride.

Language note: Papua New Guinea has many languages, not one “Papuan” language. Hunjara is an Orokaiva variety spoken in Kokoda Rural LLG. This fictional journey paraphrases exchanges in English rather than inventing local-language words; authentic phrases and pronunciation need review by local speakers. Language reference.

The crossing · Kokoda Track

Remember the people.

You carried the message through the mountains. The track was crossed by many, at terrible cost. Hunger, illness, exhaustion, rain, cold and fear took their toll alongside battle.

Papuan men and women—guides, carriers and local helpers—brought knowledge, skill and care to the campaign. The Australian War Memorial’s official history records Royal Papuan Constabulary Sergeant-Major Maga establishing a staging camp at Ioribaiwa with 130 carriers; Papuan carriers sustained the supply line; Papuan helpers also shared food and helped wounded men. Australian soldiers often called some Papuan helpers the “Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels”; that wartime nickname is only one part of their story. Their work, agency, experiences and losses belong in this history, too. Australian War Memorial, “The Japanese Advance to Kokoda”.

This fictional journey cannot tell Ted Daly’s story or name the squad mates he lost. It can only make space to remember them, and all those who endured Kokoda.

Lest we forget.
